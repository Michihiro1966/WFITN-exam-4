**Q1.** On a lateral carotid angiogram the posterior cerebral artery fills predominantly from the ICA via a large PCOM with no visible P1 segment from the basilar. Which TWO statements best describe the neurointerventional implications of this configuration? **(Select TWO.)**

a. P1 absence confirms basilar artery occlusion.  
b. This is a fetal PCA configuration, occurring in ~20–25% of individuals.  
c. Basilar artery interventions may spare the occipital lobe in this patient.  
d. The PCOM must be preserved in any ICA occlusion or flow-diverter strategy.  
e. The fetal PCA configuration is always bilateral and is never seen on one side only.

> **Answer: c, d.** A fetal-type PCA—present in ~20–25% of individuals—is defined by a dominant PCOM supplying the PCA territory with a hypoplastic or absent P1. Because the occipital lobe is supplied from the carotid side, basilar artery interventions may spare the occipital cortex in this patient (c). Conversely, ICA-side interventions (flow diverter, balloon test occlusion, parent vessel sacrifice) must preserve the PCOM to protect the dependent PCA territory (d). The fetal PCA configuration is a normal variant, not evidence of basilar occlusion. A unilateral fetal PCA is common; bilateral occurrence is less frequent but recognised.
>
> **Source:** Neuroangio.org, 'Posterior Cerebral Artery' – fetal PCA, PCOM dominance, and DSA appearance. https://neuroangio.org/anatomy-and-variants/posterior-cerebral-artery/

**Q2.** The middle cerebral artery is described as phylogenetically the youngest of all cerebral vessels. Which ONE statement best explains its embryological origin? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Direct ICA branch from the very first appearance of cerebral vessels.  
b. Develops de novo from mesodermal angioblasts in the Sylvian fissure.  
c. Originally a posterior cerebral artery branch captured by the expanding neocortex.  
d. Arose as a hypertrophied lenticulostriate vessel from the anterior cerebral artery as the neocortex expanded.  
e. Derived from the primitive olfactory artery when the olfactory lobe regressed and its territory was redistributed.

> **Answer: d.** Following Lasjaunias's phylogenetic framework (detailed on neuroangio.org), the MCA emerged from a hypertrophied lenticulostriate perforator of the ACA as the neocortex expanded. This explains why accessory MCAs occasionally arise from the A1 or ACOM region and why the MCA shows extreme branching variability—it has no single fixed ancestral template.
>
> **Source:** Neuroangio.org, 'Middle Cerebral Artery' – phylogenetic origin, lenticulostriate spectrum, and accessory MCA variants. https://neuroangio.org/anatomy-and-variants/middle-cerebral-artery/

**Q3.** A persistent primitive trigeminal artery (PTA) is identified on DSA arising from the cavernous ICA. Which TWO statements are most accurate regarding its anatomy and clinical significance? **(Select TWO.)**

a. PTA is the rarest carotid-basilar anastomosis.  
b. PTA connects the cavernous ICA to the basilar between the SCA and AICA origins.  
c. Basilar artery rostral to the PTA is perfused from the carotid side in most cases.  
d. PTA always terminates in the basilar trunk and never ends in a cerebellar artery.  
e. The posterior communicating arteries are typically enlarged when a PTA is present.

> **Answer: b, c.** The PTA is the most common persistent carotid-basilar anastomosis (~0.1–0.6% of angiograms). It arises from the cavernous ICA and joins the basilar artery between the SCA and AICA origins. Rostral to this insertion the basilar (and therefore SCA/PCA) receives carotid blood; the PCOM arteries are consequently hypoplastic. Variant PTAs may terminate in a cerebellar artery rather than the trunk itself.
>

**Q5.** During embryogenesis the ACA functioned as the rostral terminal segment of the ICA. Which ONE structure did it primarily supply in early phylogenetic development? **(Select ONE.)**

**Q6.** The persistent hypoglossal artery is the second most common carotid-basilar anastomosis. Which TWO statements correctly describe its anatomy and angiographic presentation? **(Select TWO.)**

a. It arises from the ECA and enters the cranium via the jugular foramen.  
b. Ipsilateral and often contralateral vertebral arteries are typically hypoplastic.  
c. It arises from the cervical ICA and traverses the hypoglossal canal.  
d. The basilar artery shows vertebral-type haemodynamics when this vessel is present.

> **Answer: b, c.** The persistent hypoglossal artery arises from the cervical ICA at C1–C2 level and traverses the hypoglossal (anterior condylar) canal to join the basilar artery. When present the ipsilateral—and often contralateral—vertebral arteries are hypoplastic, so the posterior fossa is effectively supplied by the carotid system. The basilar artery distal to the anastomosis exhibits carotid-type pulsatility.

**Q7.** Which ONE statement most accurately describes the embryological basis for dural venous sinus formation? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Sinuses develop from neural crest cells exclusively.  
b. Sinuses arise as direct extensions of the primitive cardiac venous system.  
c. Sinuses form through canalization of solid mesodermal cords analogous to peripheral veins.  
d. Sinuses develop from mesodermal vascular plexuses surrounding the neural tube, progressively remodelling and coalescing.  
e. Sinuses form by secondary arterialization of primitive dural channels that later lose their muscular coats and become venous.

> **Answer: d.** The dural venous sinuses develop from mesodermal vascular plexuses encircling the neural tube. An initial complex anastomotic network—the primitive head sinus and tentorial sinus system—progressively remodels, regresses, and coalesces into definitive sinuses (SSS, transverse, sigmoid, straight, cavernous). This process explains the high prevalence of anatomical variations: persistent embryonic channels, hypoplastic or absent sinuses, and asymmetric dominant drainage.
>

**Q8.** Basilar artery fenestrations are identified on DSA. Which TWO statements are most accurate regarding their developmental basis and clinical significance? **(Select TWO.)**

a. They are acquired lesions caused by atherosclerotic dissection.  
b. They most commonly occur in the proximal basilar artery.  
c. They carry a modestly increased risk of saccular aneurysm at the fenestration apex.  
d. They represent traumatic injury causing incomplete vascular healing of the posterior fossa.  
e. They are pathognomonic of a posterior circulation developmental venous anomaly.

> **Answer: b, c.** Basilar fenestrations represent incomplete fusion of the paired primitive longitudinal neural arteries—the plexiform precursor of the basilar artery. They most often occur in the proximal basilar, and their apices (the distal reunification point) are sites of increased haemodynamic stress that predispose to saccular aneurysm formation, analogous to other bifurcation sites.

**Q9.** According to the modern (NYU/Shapiro) segmental classification of the ICA, which ONE segment is defined as lying between the proximal dural ring (PDR) and the distal dural ring (DDR)? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Ophthalmic segment.  
b. Cavernous segment.  
c. Petrous horizontal segment.  
d. Transitional (clinoid) segment.

> **Answer: d.** The transitional or clinoid segment lies between the PDR (ICA exit from cavernous sinus) and the DDR (entry into the subarachnoid space). Neither ring is directly visible on DSA. This segment is the location of 'carotid cave' aneurysms—medially projecting intradural lesions just beneath the DDR—which carry rupture risk despite their apparently cavernous position on imaging.

**Q12.** A 'carotid cave' aneurysm is identified as the source of subarachnoid haemorrhage. Which ONE statement best characterises this lesion? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Intracavernous; low rupture risk; conservative management appropriate.  
b. Fusiform petrous ICA aneurysm; typically post-traumatic.

**Q14.** A persistent stapedial artery is suspected. Which ONE radiological finding is pathognomonic? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Enlarged foramen ovale.  
b. Enlarged foramen lacerum.  
c. Absent or markedly hypoplastic foramen spinosum.  
d. A bony canal visible traversing the stapes footplate on high-resolution CT.  
e. Calcification within the middle ear overlying the oval window on plain radiography.

> **Answer: c.** When the stapedial artery persists, the MMA fails to develop via its normal route through the foramen spinosum; consequently, the foramen spinosum is absent or markedly hypoplastic—the pathognomonic finding on CT or plain radiography. On DSA, the persistent stapedial artery enters the skull via the tympanic canaliculus and can be visualised as an anomalous vessel on selective ECA injection.

**Q15.** The recurrent artery of Heubner (RAH) is identified on DSA during ACOM aneurysm workup. Which TWO statements are most accurate? **(Select TWO.)**

a. It always arises from the A1 segment.  
b. Occlusion produces contralateral lower-extremity monoparesis.  
c. It most commonly arises from the A2 segment near or at the ACOM level.  
d. It supplies the medial lenticulostriate territory including the caudate head, anterior putamen, and anterior limb of the internal capsule.  
e. There is an inverse haemodynamic relationship: a large RAH correlates with smaller lateral MCA lenticulostriate arteries.

> **Answer: c, e.** The RAH most commonly arises from the proximal A2 or the ACOM and courses laterally along M1 ('recurrent') to supply the medial lenticulostriate territory. A critical inverse balance exists: a dominant RAH corresponds to smaller lateral MCA lenticulostriate vessels and vice versa. Occlusion produces a medial basal ganglia syndrome (face/arm paresis, dysarthria), not lower-extremity monoparesis which would indicate the ACA cortical territory.

**Q16.** During coiling of an ACOM aneurysm, inadvertent occlusion of which ONE small perforating vessel causes the most devastating hypothalamic injury? **(Select ONE.)**

a. The A1 segment perforators.  
b. The frontopolar artery branches.  
c. The subcallosal (median callosal) artery.  
d. Bilateral recurrent arteries of Heubner.  
e. Medial lenticulostriate arteries arising from the proximal A1 segment.

> **Answer: c.** The subcallosal artery (ACOM perforator) supplies the hypothalamus, septal nuclei, anterior commissure, and subcallosal gyrus. Neuroangio.org specifically highlights that 'inadvertent occlusion during aneurysm treatment causes devastating hypothalamic injury'—severe memory impairment, personality change, electrolyte disturbances (DI), and autonomic dysfunction. **Q19.** An anterior choroidal artery (AChA) aneurysm is being differentiated from a PCOM aneurysm on DSA. Which TWO features best support an AChA origin? **(Select TWO.)**

**Q21.** On DSA planning for PCOM aneurysm coiling, the contralateral A1 appears absent. Which TWO actions are most important before proceeding? **(Select TWO.)**

**Q26.** A small quadrigeminal plate AVM is seen supplied by a slender vessel close to the P2 segment on DSA. Which TWO statements support identifying it as the collicular (circumcollicular) artery rather than a cortical PCA branch? **(Select TWO.)**

**Q27.** The superior cerebellar artery (SCA) has important relationships to cranial nerves. Which TWO statements are most accurate for planning posterior fossa interventions? **(Select TWO.)**

a. SCA runs between CN III and CN VII around the midbrain.  
b. SCA duplication requires treatment when identified because it predisposes to aneurysm.  
c. SCA is the most constant of the three paired cerebellar arteries in terms of origin and territory.  
d. SCA aneurysms at the basilar–SCA junction may compress CN III or CN IV due to the SCA's course between them.  
e. The AICA is the most constant cerebellar artery and is always found at the same level of the basilar artery.

> **Answer: c, d.** The SCA is the most anatomically constant of the cerebellar arteries. It arises from the rostral basilar just below the bifurcation and courses between CN III (above) and CN IV (below) around the midbrain. **Q28.** A posterior communicating artery (PCOM) aneurysm is treated by endovascular coiling. Which TWO statements are most relevant to procedural planning? **(Select TWO.)**

a. PCOM aneurysms invariably project superiorly and are never associated with CN III palsy.

c. An infundibular dilatation (≤3 mm, with a branch from the apex) is generally treated as an aneurysm.  
d. PCOM aneurysms arise from the posterolateral wall of the ICA and typically project posterolaterally or inferolaterally.

> **Answer: b, d.** PCOM aneurysms arise from the posterolateral ICA wall at the PCOM origin, projecting posterolaterally or inferolaterally, and can compress CN III causing oculomotor palsy. Neuroangio.org emphasises preservation of fetal PCOM when planning flow diversion or ICA sacrifice.

**Q30.** The MMA and ophthalmic artery are in haemodynamic 'balance.' Which ONE variant describes the MMA providing the complete orbital and ophthalmic arterial supply, including the central retinal artery? **(Select ONE.)**

b. Meningo-orbital variant.  
c. Complete meningo-ophthalmic variant.  
d. Petrosquamosal orbital branch variant.

**Q31.** MMA embolization for a chronic subdural haematoma is being planned. Which TWO steps are essential to prevent catastrophic visual complications? **(Select TWO.)**

d. Obtain optic nerve ultrasound to confirm retinal perfusion before embolization.  
e. Use only large particles (>500 µm) regardless of the anastomotic pattern identified on angiography.

> **Answer: b, c.** Central retinal artery occlusion causing irreversible blindness is the most feared complication of MMA embolization. Pre-procedure selective MMA DSA must include evaluation for meningolacrimal, meningo-orbital, or complete meningo-ophthalmic anastomoses. **Q32.** On selective DSA a vessel resembling the vertebral artery is seen arising from the external carotid artery territory at the skull base. Which TWO options are plausible explanations? **(Select TWO.)**

**Q33.** During embolization of a skull-base meningioma supplied by the ascending pharyngeal artery, two divisions are identified: the hypoglossal and jugular divisions of the neuromeningeal trunk. Which TWO statements are most accurate? **(Select TWO.)**

b. The hypoglossal division enters the hypoglossal canal and supplies CN XII and adjacent dura.  
c. The jugular division enters the jugular foramen and supplies CN IX, X, XI and the adjacent dura.  
d. Both divisions can be safely embolised with particles without cranial nerve testing.  
e. Anastomosis with the MHT via the clivus makes embolization from either division safe without fluoroscopy.

> **Answer: b, c.** The neuromeningeal trunk divides into the hypoglossal division (hypoglossal canal → CN XII supply, dura, and anastomosis with contralateral side around the odontoid) and the jugular division (jugular foramen → CN IX/X/XI supply and adjacent dura). **Q34.** On late-phase DSA the straight sinus (sinus rectus) is formed by the junction of which ONE combination of structures? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Basal vein of Rosenthal and the internal cerebral vein directly.  
b. Inferior sagittal sinus and the basal vein of Rosenthal.  
c. Superior sagittal sinus and the bilateral internal cerebral veins.  
d. Lateral mesencephalic vein and the inferior sagittal sinus.

> **Answer: e.** The straight sinus forms at the junction of the inferior sagittal sinus and the vein of Galen. The vein of Galen is itself formed by confluence of the bilateral internal cerebral veins and the basal veins of Rosenthal at the quadrigeminal cistern. The straight sinus drains posteriorly into the torcular Herophili. This anatomy is critical for planning treatment of vein of Galen malformations, deep AVMs, and deep cerebral venous thrombosis.

**Q35.** The basal vein of Rosenthal (BVR) is described on neuroangio.org as a 'surface vein on the inferior aspect of the brain.' Which TWO statements are most accurate? **(Select TWO.)**

a. The BVR drains the posterior corpus callosum exclusively.  
b. The BVR receives the anterior cerebral vein, deep middle cerebral vein, inferior striate veins, and uncal vein.  
c. The BVR courses through the ambient cistern around the midbrain and drains into the vein of Galen.  
d. The BVR is visible only on vertebral artery injection, never on carotid injection.  
e. Occlusion of the BVR produces a superficial cortical venous infarct confined to the temporal lobe.

> **Answer: b, c.** The BVR receives multiple tributaries (anterior cerebral vein, deep middle cerebral vein draining the insula/basal ganglia, inferior striate veins, uncal vein, and lateral mesencephalic vein). **Q36.** Regarding developmental venous anomalies (DVAs), which TWO statements are most critical for neurointerventional decision-making? **(Select TWO.)**

a. DVAs should be embolised to eliminate future haemorrhage risk.  
b. DVAs represent primitive arterial developmental remnants.  
c. DVAs remain visible into very late venous phases on DSA due to prolonged deep white matter capillary transit.  
d. DVAs are the sole venous drainage for their territory; sacrifice causes venous infarction.  
e. DVAs can be safely included in a resection margin because they represent non-functional tissue.

> **Answer: c, d.** DVAs represent the sole venous drainage for their territory—sacrifice or occlusion inevitably causes venous infarction. They must be preserved at surgery and cannot be safely embolised. On DSA, DVAs characteristically persist into very late venous phases due to prolonged capillary transit in deep white matter. Their association with adjacent cavernous malformations in the same drainage territory (to be sought on MRI) is well-established.

**Q37.** Transvenous embolisation of a cavernous sinus dAVF is planned but the ipsilateral inferior petrosal sinus (IPS) appears occluded. Which TWO alternative venous access routes may be used? **(Select TWO.)**

**Q39.** The artery of Adamkiewicz is being identified before thoracolumbar surgery. Which TWO statements best describe its typical characteristics? **(Select TWO.)**

a. Arises from L1–L3 on the right side in ~60% of cases.  
b. Has a characteristic 'hairpin' turn as it joins the anterior spinal artery.  
c. Arises most commonly from T8–L2 on the left side in approximately 75% of cases.  
d. Is always the largest intercostal artery visible at a given thoracic level.  
e. Occlusion produces posterior cord syndrome with selective loss of position sense and vibration.

> **Answer: b, c.** The artery of Adamkiewicz (arteria radicularis magna) arises from segmental arteries at T8–L2, most frequently T9–T12, with a strong left-side predominance (~75%). On spinal DSA it is identified by its pathognomonic 'hairpin' turn joining the anterior spinal artery, ascending before the descending ASA continues caudally. Occlusion causes anterior cord syndrome (bilateral paralysis, loss of pain/temperature sensation) with preserved posterior column function—not posterior cord syndrome.

**Q40.** On cerebral DSA, which TWO statements best reflect current principles of frame rate selection for optimising diagnostic quality and minimising radiation? **(Select TWO.)**

a. Maximum frame rate (15+ fps) should always be used for all cerebrovascular DSA.  
b. Frame rate selection has no effect on radiation dose or spatial resolution.  
c. Increasing frame rate increases radiation dose and decreases per-frame spatial resolution.  
d. Lower frame rates (2–4 fps) provide superior information per unit of radiation for routine diagnostic DSA.  
e. High frame rates (≥6 fps) are appropriate only when rapid flow dynamics need to be characterised, such as in direct CCF or high-flow AVM.

> **Answer: c, d.** As detailed on neuroangio.org, increasing frame rate raises radiation dose while reducing per-frame resolution (less radiation per frame → lower SNR). For routine diagnostic cerebrovascular angiography, 2–4 fps maximises spatial resolution per unit of radiation—providing more diagnostic information per dose. High frame rates are justified only when temporal resolution is specifically required: direct CCF, high-flow AVM, or precise AV shunt timing.
