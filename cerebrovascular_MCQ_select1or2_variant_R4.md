## Section I: Vascular Neuroembryology and Development

---

**Q1.** During balloon test occlusion of the cervical ICA, lateral angiography shows that the ipsilateral PCA territory depends almost entirely on a large PCOM, while the ipsilateral P1 is not seen from the vertebrobasilar injection. Which TWO statements are most appropriate for treatment planning? **(Select TWO.)**

a. The finding proves an acute P1 thromboembolic occlusion.  
b. This configuration is a fetal-type PCA and may be unilateral.  
c. ICA sacrifice or flow diversion must account for PCA-territory dependence on the PCOM.  
d. Basilar artery occlusion would necessarily infarct the ipsilateral occipital cortex first.  
e. The PCOM can be ignored because it is only an embryonic remnant in adults.

> **Answer: b, c.** A fetal-type PCA is characterized by a dominant PCOM supplying the PCA territory with a hypoplastic or absent P1 segment. It is often unilateral. ICA-side treatment—including parent artery occlusion, flow diversion, or prolonged balloon inflation—can compromise the occipital PCA territory if the PCOM is not preserved.

---

**Q2.** Which ONE developmental concept best explains why the MCA has highly variable branching and occasional accessory origins from the A1/ACOM region? **(Select ONE.)**

a. The MCA is the oldest direct terminal branch of the primitive ICA.  
b. The MCA derives from the primitive hypoglossal artery.  
c. The MCA develops by fusion of paired longitudinal neural arteries.  
d. The MCA arose from an ACA-related lenticulostriate system that enlarged with neocortical expansion.  
e. The MCA represents persistence of the primitive olfactory artery after regression of the ACA.

> **Answer: d.** The MCA is phylogenetically young and is understood as arising from an ACA-associated lenticulostriate system that hypertrophied as the neocortex expanded. This explains accessory MCA patterns and the broad spectrum of MCA branching variability.

---

**Q3.** A persistent primitive trigeminal artery (PTA) is found during workup of a posterior circulation aneurysm. Which TWO statements are correct? **(Select TWO.)**

a. It usually arises from the cervical ICA at C1–C2.  
b. It typically connects the cavernous ICA with the basilar artery between the SCA and AICA levels.  
c. The basilar artery rostral to the anastomosis may be supplied from the carotid circulation.  
d. The ipsilateral PCOM is usually enlarged because the PTA increases posterior communicating flow.  
e. A PTA always terminates in the basilar trunk and never in an SCA or AICA-type branch.

> **Answer: b, c.** The PTA usually arises from the cavernous ICA and joins the basilar artery between SCA and AICA. The rostral basilar system, including SCA and PCA territories, may receive carotid-side supply. PCOMs are often hypoplastic rather than enlarged, and cerebellar-branch variants exist.

**Q4.** A single unpaired A2 trunk supplies both anterior cerebral artery territories. Which TWO statements best describe this finding? **(Select TWO.)**

a. It is common, occurring in roughly 10% of angiograms.  
b. It is a duplicated A1 segment and has no aneurysm association.  
c. It is an azygous ACA configuration.  
d. Occlusion would usually produce only unilateral foot weakness.  
e. Terminal/pericallosal aneurysm formation is relatively overrepresented because bilateral ACA flow is carried by one trunk.

> **Answer: c, e.** The azygous ACA is a single A2 trunk supplying both hemispheres. It is uncommon and clinically important because the single distal trunk is hemodynamically stressed and is associated with terminal/pericallosal aneurysms. Occlusion can compromise bilateral medial frontal territories.

**Q5.** In the phylogenetic framework of cerebral artery development, the early ACA primarily supplied which ONE territory? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Occipital cortex and calcarine fissure.  
b. Lateral temporal cortex and insula.  
c. Cerebellum and dorsal brainstem.  
d. Olfactory lobe and basal ganglia precursor region.  
e. The entire neocortex before formation of the posterior cerebral artery.

> **Answer: d.** The ACA is among the phylogenetically older cerebral vessels and initially served the rostral terminal ICA territory, including the olfactory lobe and basal ganglia precursors. This context helps explain Heubner, infraoptic A1, and accessory MCA variants.

**Q6.** Selective carotid injection shows a large vessel arising from the high cervical ICA, passing through the hypoglossal canal, and supplying the basilar artery. Which TWO statements are correct? **(Select TWO.)**

a. This is a persistent stapedial artery.  
b. The vertebral arteries are often small or hypoplastic.  
c. The vessel is a persistent hypoglossal artery.  
d. It typically arises from the ECA and enters via the jugular foramen.  
e. Its presence excludes carotid-type supply of the posterior fossa.

> **Answer: b, c.** A persistent hypoglossal artery arises from the cervical ICA, traverses the hypoglossal canal, and joins the basilar artery. The ipsilateral and sometimes contralateral vertebral arteries may be hypoplastic, so posterior fossa perfusion may be carotid-dependent.

**Q7.** Which ONE statement best describes formation of the dural venous sinuses during development? **(Select ONE.)**

a. They arise exclusively from neural crest-derived arterial channels.  
b. They are direct extensions of the primitive jugular bulb without remodeling.  
c. They form by ossification of primitive skull-base venous canals.  
d. They develop from vascular plexuses around the neural tube that remodel, regress, and coalesce into definitive sinuses.  
e. They are secondary low-pressure channels created only after the pial veins mature.

> **Answer: d.** Dural venous sinuses develop from primitive vascular plexuses surrounding the neural tube. Remodeling and coalescence of these plexuses produce the superior sagittal, transverse, sigmoid, straight, cavernous, and related sinuses, explaining the high frequency of venous variants.

**Q8.** A proximal basilar fenestration is seen adjacent to a small saccular aneurysm. Which TWO statements are most accurate? **(Select TWO.)**

a. Basilar fenestration is usually an acquired result of healed vertebrobasilar dissection.  
b. Fenestrations reflect incomplete fusion of paired primitive longitudinal neural arteries.  
c. The proximal basilar artery is a common location.  
d. Fenestrations are pathognomonic for a developmental venous anomaly.  
e. Aneurysms preferentially occur at the proximal vertebral artery origin, not at the fenestration apex.

> **Answer: b, c.** Basilar fenestrations represent incomplete fusion of the paired primitive longitudinal neural arteries. They are commonly proximal, and hemodynamic stress at the fenestration ends—especially the reunification apex—may predispose to saccular aneurysm.

## Section II: Internal Carotid Artery

---

**Q9.** In the modern segmental classification of the ICA, which ONE segment lies between the proximal and distal dural rings? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Petrous segment.  
b. Lacerum segment.  
c. Cavernous segment.  
d. Transitional/clinoid segment.  
e. Communicating segment.

> **Answer: d.** The transitional or clinoid ICA segment lies between the proximal dural ring and distal dural ring. This distinction matters because lesions near the dural rings may be intradural despite seeming cavernous on projection angiography.

**Q10.** The meningohypophyseal trunk (MHT) is selected during evaluation of a tentorial dural AVF. Which TWO statements are correct? **(Select TWO.)**

a. The MHT usually arises from the petrous ICA.  
b. The MHT commonly arises from the posterior genu of the cavernous ICA.  
c. The tentorial artery of Bernasconi-Cassinari may be a major feeder to tentorial dAVFs.  
d. The inferior hypophyseal branch mainly supplies the adenohypophysis.  
e. The MHT has no relevant dural supply and is only a pituitary vessel.

> **Answer: b, c.** The MHT usually arises from the posterior genu of the cavernous ICA. Its tentorial branch is an important feeder to tentorial dural AVFs and skull-base tumors. The inferior hypophyseal branch supplies the posterior pituitary rather than the anterior pituitary.

**Q11.** During embolization planning for a middle cranial fossa meningioma, the inferolateral trunk (ILT) is recognized as a potential dangerous anastomotic pathway. Which TWO statements are correct? **(Select TWO.)**

a. The ILT supplies only the posterior pituitary.  
b. The ILT can supply the cavernous cranial nerves and middle cranial fossa floor.  
c. The ILT is isolated from the ECA system and therefore safe to embolize blindly.  
d. The ILT cannot participate in ophthalmic artery variants.  
e. The ILT may anastomose with the MMA and accessory meningeal arteries through skull-base foramina.

> **Answer: b, e.** The ILT supplies the cavernous sinus walls, cranial nerves III, IV, V1, V2/V3-related territories, VI, and middle cranial fossa floor. It communicates with the ECA system, especially MMA and accessory meningeal branches, so it is a critical safety consideration during embolization.

**Q12.** A small medial ICA aneurysm near the anterior clinoid appears extradural on a single projection, but SAH pattern suggests rupture. Which ONE description best fits a carotid cave aneurysm? **(Select ONE.)**

a. A fusiform traumatic petrous ICA aneurysm.  
b. A cavernous aneurysm with essentially no SAH risk.  
c. A medially projecting intradural aneurysm arising in the carotid cave just beneath the distal dural ring.  
d. An ICA terminus aneurysm at the A1–M1 bifurcation.  
e. A dissecting aneurysm of the cervical ICA.

> **Answer: c.** A carotid cave aneurysm arises from the medial ICA wall in a subarachnoid recess just beneath the distal dural ring. It may appear deceptively cavernous or clinoid on routine angiography, but it is intradural and therefore has rupture risk.

**Q13.** A patient has conductive hearing symptoms and a red pulsatile retrotympanic mass. Angiography suggests an aberrant ICA. Which TWO statements are correct? **(Select TWO.)**

a. It is a benign middle-ear polyp and biopsy is diagnostically appropriate.  
b. It represents duplication of the petrous ICA.  
c. It results from failure of normal cervical ICA development with reconstitution through inferior tympanic–caroticotympanic channels.  
d. The aberrant vessel courses through the middle ear before reconstituting the petrous ICA.  
e. CT typically shows a normal carotid canal with a separate venous canal through the cochlea.

> **Answer: c, d.** Aberrant ICA reflects failed normal cervical ICA development with collateral reconstitution through the inferior tympanic and caroticotympanic arteries. The vessel follows an abnormal middle-ear course and biopsy can cause catastrophic hemorrhage.

**Q14.** Which ONE imaging sign most strongly supports the diagnosis of persistent stapedial artery? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Enlarged foramen lacerum.  
b. Enlarged jugular foramen.  
c. Absent or markedly hypoplastic foramen spinosum.  
d. Persistent trigeminal artery entering Meckel's cave.  
e. A duplicated hypoglossal canal.

> **Answer: c.** Persistent stapedial artery alters normal MMA development; the MMA does not pass normally through the foramen spinosum. Therefore, absent or markedly hypoplastic foramen spinosum is the key CT clue.

## Section III: Anterior Circulation

---

**Q15.** During ACOM aneurysm treatment planning, the recurrent artery of Heubner is seen arising near the ACOM/proximal A2 region. Which TWO statements best describe its anatomy and risk? **(Select TWO.)**

a. It always arises from the distal A2 segment beyond the callosomarginal artery.  
b. Its occlusion mainly causes isolated ipsilateral visual field loss.  
c. It commonly arises from the proximal A2 or ACOM region and courses recurrently toward the MCA.  
d. It supplies medial lenticulostriate territory including caudate head, anterior putamen, and anterior limb of the internal capsule.  
e. Its presence means ACOM perforators can be sacrificed safely.

> **Answer: c, d.** The recurrent artery of Heubner usually arises around the ACOM/proximal A2 region and courses laterally/recurrently. It supplies medial basal ganglia and internal capsule territories; injury can cause deep infarction and motor/cognitive deficits.

---

**Q16.** Which ONE perforating branch is particularly feared during ACOM aneurysm coiling because its occlusion can cause severe hypothalamic and memory/autonomic disturbance? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Frontopolar artery.  
b. Orbitofrontal artery.  
c. Subcallosal/median callosal artery.  
d. Distal pericallosal artery.  
e. Callosomarginal artery.

> **Answer: c.** The subcallosal or median callosal artery is a critical ACOM perforator supplying hypothalamic and septal regions. Its occlusion may cause severe memory impairment, personality change, endocrine/autonomic disturbance, and poor recovery.

---

**Q17.** Which TWO statements regarding MCA lenticulostriate arteries are most useful during M1 thrombectomy or stenting? **(Select TWO.)**

a. They have abundant pial collateral supply, so brief M1 perforator compromise is harmless.  
b. Their number and caliber are fixed at 4 vessels in almost all adults.  
c. A dominant lateral lenticulostriate system tends to correspond to a smaller Heubner contribution.  
d. They behave as functional end arteries, so perforator occlusion can infarct deep structures despite good cortical collaterals.  
e. They primarily supply the cerebellar dentate nucleus.

> **Answer: c, d.** MCA lenticulostriates are functional end arteries supplying basal ganglia and internal capsule territories. Their caliber and number vary, and their distribution is in inverse balance with the medial lenticulostriate/Heubner system.

---

**Q18.** An additional MCA-like artery arises from the A1/ACOM region and supplies part of the frontal opercular territory. Which TWO interpretations are most appropriate? **(Select TWO.)**

a. It is always a pathologic AVM feeder.  
b. Accessory MCA variants may arise from A1 or the ACOM region.  
c. Accessory and duplicated MCA patterns reflect the developmental continuum between ACA lenticulostriate and MCA systems.  
d. It guarantees complete redundancy of the MCA territory during thrombectomy.  
e. MCA fenestration, if present, mandates embolization before aneurysm treatment.

> **Answer: b, c.** Accessory MCA variants may arise from A1 or the ACOM region and reflect the developmental spectrum of MCA formation from ACA-associated lenticulostriate precursors. They are not necessarily pathological but must be recognized during catheter navigation.

**Q19.** In differentiating an anterior choroidal artery aneurysm from a PCOM aneurysm, which TWO features support an AChA origin? **(Select TWO.)**

a. The aneurysm arises proximal to the PCOM origin.  
b. The aneurysm projects laterally from the carotid bifurcation.  
c. The lesion is distal to the PCOM origin.  
d. It projects inferomedially toward the sella on frontal projection.  
e. A fetal PCA on the same side proves the aneurysm is from the PCOM.

> **Answer: c, d.** AChA aneurysms arise distal to the PCOM origin and often project inferomedially. This projection and level help distinguish them from PCOM aneurysms, which arise at the PCOM origin and classically project posterolaterally/inferolaterally.

**Q20.** An infraoptic A1 is identified before a suprasellar/ACOM operation. Which ONE statement is most accurate? **(Select ONE.)**

a. It is a normal high-riding A1 above the optic chiasm.  
b. It is caused by vertebral-basilar anastomosis persistence.  
c. It has no operative significance because it never enters the surgical corridor.  
d. It represents an enlarged recurrent artery of Heubner.  
e. It reflects persistence of an embryonic ophthalmic pathway, with the A1 passing below the optic nerve and creating a surgical hazard.

> **Answer: e.** Infraoptic A1 places the ACA in an unexpectedly low course beneath the optic nerve/chiasmal region. It is embryologically linked to ophthalmic artery developmental pathways and is important in ACOM and suprasellar approaches.

**Q21.** On an ipsilateral ICA injection for PCOM aneurysm planning, the opposite A1 is not visualized. Which TWO steps help avoid a dangerous misinterpretation? **(Select TWO.)**

a. Assume true aplasia and proceed without additional angiography.  
b. Perform contralateral ICA injection to evaluate ACOM cross-filling.  
c. Use cross-compression or similar maneuvers when needed to clarify hemodynamic dependence.  
d. Proceed with ICA sacrifice because one A1 is always enough.  
e. Treat absent A1 as an absolute contraindication to all endovascular therapy.

> **Answer: b, c.** Apparent A1 absence may reflect flow dominance and cross-filling rather than true aplasia. Contralateral injection and, when appropriate, compression maneuvers can define the ACOM complex and whether one A1 supplies both A2 territories.

## Section IV: Posterior Circulation

---

**Q22.** Vertebral angiography shows marked left–right caliber asymmetry without dissection signs. Which TWO statements are most accurate? **(Select TWO.)**

a. The right vertebral artery is dominant in most people.  
b. Vertebral asymmetry alone is a common developmental variant.  
c. Left vertebral dominance is more frequent than right dominance.  
d. A small transverse foramen always proves congenital vertebral hypoplasia.  
e. Asymmetry is synonymous with atherosclerotic stenosis.

> **Answer: b, c.** Vertebral artery asymmetry is common and often developmental. Left vertebral dominance is frequent. Foraminal size is a helpful clue but cannot alone exclude acquired pathology or define functional dominance.

---

**Q23.** A vessel from the thyrocervical region enters the transverse foramen around C4 and resembles the vertebral artery. Which ONE vessel is the most likely mimic? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Occipital artery.  
b. Middle meningeal artery.  
c. Posterior meningeal artery.  
d. Ascending cervical artery.  
e. Superior thyroid artery.

> **Answer: d.** The ascending cervical artery, from the thyrocervical system, can enter at C4–C5 and mimic a vertebral artery. Careful tracing to its origin is essential before cervical or spinal procedures.

**Q24.** A PICA origin is extradural. Which TWO statements are important for treatment planning? **(Select TWO.)**

a. Extradural PICA origin makes the intradural VA non-eloquent.  
b. Extradural PICA cannot supply the usual lateral medullary perforators.  
c. PICA and AICA territories are fixed and never compensate for each other.  
d. Lateral medullary perforators must arise from the intradural VA in this configuration.  
e. Extradural PICA origin eliminates risk of medullary infarction during VA procedures.

> **Answer: b, d.** When PICA arises extradurally, it cannot give the usual intradural lateral medullary perforators. Therefore, these perforators must arise from the intradural vertebral artery, making that segment highly eloquent.

**Q25.** During embolization of an ascending pharyngeal artery supply to a skull-base tumor, the odontoid arcade is identified. Which ONE statement is correct? **(Select ONE.)**

a. It is a venous plexus around the dens and has no embolization relevance.  
b. It is a PICA–PICA arterial arcade at the cerebellomedullary fissure.  
c. It is an ophthalmic–MMA anastomosis through the superior orbital fissure.  
d. It is an anastomotic pathway from the hypoglossal division of the ascending pharyngeal artery to the vertebral artery near C2–C3, creating risk of posterior fossa embolization.  
e. It is a terminal branch of the SCA to the trochlear nerve.

> **Answer: d.** The odontoid arcade connects branches of the ascending pharyngeal system near the hypoglossal canal/odontoid region with the vertebral artery. Embolic material can reach the posterior circulation, so superselective positioning and safety testing are crucial.

**Q26.** A small quadrigeminal plate AVM is supplied by a thin artery arising near P2. Which TWO findings support a collicular/circumcollicular artery rather than a routine cortical PCA branch? **(Select TWO.)**

a. It is larger than the PCA trunk.  
b. It may be invisible on nonselective vertebral injection.  
c. It supplies only the distal calcarine cortex.  
d. It can be embolized like an inferior temporal branch without perforator concern.  
e. Superselective angiography may show a course around the midbrain with adjacent peduncular perforators.

> **Answer: b, e.** The collicular/circumcollicular artery is a small perforator-like branch near P2 that may not be evident on nonselective injection. Superselective angiography helps define its midbrain-adjacent course and dangerous perforator relationships.

**Q27.** Which TWO statements about the superior cerebellar artery (SCA) are most relevant near the basilar apex? **(Select TWO.)**

a. It normally courses below CN VII and above CN XII.  
b. Duplication of the SCA is itself an indication for prophylactic treatment.  
c. It is relatively constant among the cerebellar arteries in origin and territory.  
d. Basilar–SCA junction aneurysms may affect CN III or CN IV because of the SCA course around the midbrain.  
e. AICA is always more constant than SCA and never varies in size.

> **Answer: c, d.** The SCA is the most constant of the cerebellar arteries and arises near the basilar apex. Its course around the midbrain places it in close relation to CN III and CN IV, relevant for aneurysms and surgical/endovascular planning.

**Q28.** A PCOM aneurysm is being prepared for coiling. Which TWO statements best guide interpretation and treatment planning? **(Select TWO.)**

a. PCOM aneurysms always project superiorly and therefore do not cause CN III palsy.  
b. PCOM aneurysms have relatively high recurrence after coiling compared with some other locations.  
c. A true infundibulum with a small conical origin and branch from the apex is generally not treated as an aneurysm.  
d. PCOM aneurysms arise from the posterolateral ICA wall at the PCOM origin and commonly project posterolaterally/inferolaterally.  
e. In a fetal PCA, the PCOM can be sacrificed safely because the P1 will always enlarge immediately.

> **Answer: b, d.** PCOM aneurysms arise at the PCOM origin from the posterolateral ICA and often project posterolaterally or inferolaterally. They are associated with relatively high recurrence after coiling. A fetal PCA requires PCOM preservation rather than sacrifice.

**Q29.** A microcatheter is positioned in the jugular division of the ascending pharyngeal artery during glomus jugulare embolization. Which TWO statements are correct? **(Select TWO.)**

a. This division supplies the optic chiasm and retina.  
b. It may directly supply CN IX, X, and XI, making embolization high-risk.  
c. Provocative lidocaine testing may help detect cranial nerve supply before particle or liquid embolization.  
d. It is safe to embolize because cranial nerves receive only pial arterial supply.  
e. It has no clinically relevant skull-base or posterior fossa anastomoses.

> **Answer: b, c.** The jugular division of the ascending pharyngeal artery supplies lower cranial nerves IX, X, and XI and adjacent dura. Provocative testing and very careful superselective angiography are important safety steps.

**Q30.** Which ONE MMA–ophthalmic variant is defined by the MMA supplying the entire orbital/ophthalmic circulation, including the central retinal artery? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Meningolacrimal variant.  
b. Recurrent meningeal variant.  
c. Complete meningo-ophthalmic variant.  
d. Accessory meningeal variant without orbital supply.  
e. Petrosal branch variant.

> **Answer: c.** In a complete meningo-ophthalmic variant, the MMA supplies the full ophthalmic territory including central retinal supply. This is rare but critical because MMA embolization could cause blindness.

**Q31.** For MMA embolization of chronic subdural hematoma, which TWO angiographic precautions are essential to reduce visual risk? **(Select TWO.)**

a. Skip selective MMA angiography if the catheter is stable.  
b. Perform selective MMA injection to look for ophthalmic artery filling.  
c. Exclude meningolacrimal/meningo-orbital/complete meningo-ophthalmic anastomoses before embolic injection.  
d. Treat the central retinal artery with prophylactic heparin injection through the microcatheter.  
e. Use the same embolic material regardless of any MMA–ophthalmic anastomosis.

> **Answer: b, c.** Selective MMA angiography must assess for orbital or retinal supply through MMA–ophthalmic anastomoses. If ophthalmic territory fills, the embolization strategy must be changed to avoid central retinal artery occlusion and blindness.

**Q32.** On ECA injection, a skull-base vessel appears to reconstitute the vertebrobasilar system. Which TWO anatomical explanations are plausible? **(Select TWO.)**

a. A normal vertebral artery origin from the ECA, which is common.  
b. Ascending pharyngeal artery reconstitution through the hypoglossal canal.  
c. Occipital artery proatlantal-type carotid–vertebral anastomosis.  
d. A middle meningeal artery directly entering the basilar artery through foramen spinosum.  
e. Superior thyroid artery continuation as the basilar artery.

> **Answer: b, c.** The ascending pharyngeal artery may reconstitute vertebrobasilar flow through the hypoglossal canal, and the occipital artery may form a proatlantal-type anastomosis. Both are critical to recognize before ECA embolization.

**Q33.** The hypoglossal and jugular divisions of the neuromeningeal trunk are seen during ascending pharyngeal artery angiography. Which TWO statements are correct? **(Select TWO.)**

a. The hypoglossal division supplies CN II and III.  
b. The hypoglossal division enters the hypoglossal canal and supplies CN XII and adjacent dura.

## Section VI: Cerebral Venous Anatomy

---

**Q34.** On late venous phase DSA, the straight sinus is formed by which ONE junction? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Superior sagittal sinus and transverse sinus.  
b. Basal vein of Rosenthal and cavernous sinus.  
c. Inferior petrosal sinus and sigmoid sinus.  
d. Internal cerebral vein and superior sagittal sinus.  
e. Inferior sagittal sinus and vein of Galen.

> **Answer: e.** The straight sinus forms where the inferior sagittal sinus meets the vein of Galen. The vein of Galen is formed by the deep venous system including internal cerebral veins and basal veins of Rosenthal.

---

**Q35.** Which TWO statements about the basal vein of Rosenthal are correct? **(Select TWO.)**

a. It drains only the posterior corpus callosum.  
b. It receives deep tributaries such as anterior cerebral vein, deep middle cerebral vein, inferior striate veins, and uncal vein.  
c. It courses through the ambient cistern around the midbrain toward the vein of Galen.  
d. It is seen only on vertebral artery injection and never on carotid injection.  
e. Its occlusion produces a purely superficial convexity venous infarct.

> **Answer: b, c.** The basal vein of Rosenthal receives multiple deep and basal tributaries and courses around the midbrain in the ambient cistern to drain toward the vein of Galen. It is a major deep/basal venous pathway.

---

**Q36.** Which TWO principles about developmental venous anomalies (DVAs) are most important during surgery or embolization? **(Select TWO.)**

a. DVAs should be embolized to prevent future hemorrhage.  
b. DVAs are arterial embryonic remnants.  
c. DVAs may persist into very late venous phase on DSA.  
d. DVAs are functional sole-drainage pathways; occlusion can cause venous infarction.  
e. DVAs can be sacrificed safely if the associated cavernous malformation is removed.

> **Answer: c, d.** DVAs are venous drainage routes for otherwise normal brain tissue. They often have delayed venous-phase opacification and must be preserved; occlusion or surgical sacrifice can cause venous infarction.

---

**Q37.** Cavernous sinus dAVF embolization is planned, but the ipsilateral IPS is not catheterizable. Which TWO alternative venous access routes may be considered? **(Select TWO.)**

a. Treatment must be abandoned.  
b. Facial vein → angular vein → superior ophthalmic vein route.  
c. Facial/deep facial venous route to the pterygoid plexus, or direct pterygoid/puncture route in selected cases.  
d. Direct carotid arterial puncture into the cavernous sinus is the standard next step.  
e. Dilated superior ophthalmic vein always contraindicates transorbital access.

> **Answer: b, c.** When the IPS is occluded, cavernous sinus access may be possible through the superior ophthalmic vein pathway or the pterygoid plexus/deep facial venous route, depending on venous anatomy and operator experience.

---

**Q38.** Which ONE deep venous tributary may remain opacified very late, even after the dural sinuses have cleared on ICA venography? **(Select ONE.)**

a. Superior sagittal sinus.  
b. Cortical bridging vein over the precentral gyrus.  
c. Straight sinus near the torcular.  
d. Superficial middle cerebral vein.  
e. Caudate vein draining toward the internal cerebral vein.

> **Answer: e.** The caudate vein can remain visible extremely late because of prolonged transit through deep periventricular/basal ganglia tissue. This normal phenomenon should not be mistaken for venous obstruction.

## Section VII: Spinal Vascular Anatomy and DSA Technique

---

**Q39.** The artery of Adamkiewicz is sought before thoracoabdominal aortic or thoracolumbar spine surgery. Which TWO statements are typical? **(Select TWO.)**

a. It most often arises from the right L2–L5 levels.  
b. It has a characteristic hairpin configuration as it joins the anterior spinal artery.  
c. It most commonly arises from T8–L2, often on the left side.  
d. It always corresponds to the largest intercostal artery.  
e. Its occlusion causes pure posterior column dysfunction with preserved motor power.

> **Answer: b, c.** The artery of Adamkiewicz most commonly arises between T8 and L2 with left-sided predominance. The characteristic hairpin turn joining the anterior spinal artery is the key angiographic sign. Occlusion risks anterior cord syndrome.

---

**Q40.** For routine diagnostic cerebral DSA, which TWO statements best describe frame-rate selection? **(Select TWO.)**

a. The highest available frame rate should be used for every diagnostic run.  
b. Frame rate has no effect on radiation dose.  
c. Higher frame rates increase radiation dose and can reduce per-frame image quality.  
d. Lower frame rates such as 2–4 fps are often sufficient and dose-efficient for routine diagnostic angiography.  
e. High frame rate is mandatory for all chronic subdural MMA embolization angiograms.

> **Answer: c, d.** Increasing frame rate increases dose and may reduce per-frame signal-to-noise/resolution. For routine diagnostic DSA, lower frame rates usually provide adequate temporal information with better dose efficiency; higher frame rates are reserved for rapid-flow questions.
